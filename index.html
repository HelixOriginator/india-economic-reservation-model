HOW CLASSIFICATION WORKS

Household financial audit, not birth-based category

Every applicant runs through the same two-stage test: hard exclusion rules first, then a tiered income threshold. The result is a dynamically computed category rather than a fixed, inherited one.

HOUSEHOLD FINANCIAL AUDIT

Hard Exclusions Met?

  • Father or mother is/was a Class-I Gazetted Officer
  • Household property totals ≥ 2,000 sq ft
  • Household holds active passive income
→ GENERAL / UNRESERVED

Under Income Threshold?

  • Household income < ₹4 Lakhs → ST Category
  • Household income < ₹6 Lakhs → SC Category
  • Household income < ₹9 Lakhs → OBC Category
→ RESERVED CATEGORY

CONSTITUTIONAL CONTEXT

Legal & constitutional dimensions

The proposed model departs from the prevailing basis for reservation and would require a distinct constitutional pathway.

Policy DimensionPREVAILING
Current System		PROPOSED
Reform Model
Primary Basis Social backwardness & historical caste lineage (Articles 15 & 16) Economic status, household asset caps, and multi-tier income limits
Creamy Layer Scope Applied only to OBC (₹8L) and EWS categories, per the Indra Sawhney precedent Applied universally across all tiers — ST, SC, and OBC — to prevent privilege hoarding
General Category Disadvantage Addressed partially via the 10% EWS quota (Janhit Abhiyan, 2022) Low-income General category households qualify directly for the lower-income tiers
Constitutional Pathway Existing Articles 15(4), 16(4), 341, and 342 Would require constitutional amendment to Articles 15/16 and 341/342, or a sub-classification framework

This section summarizes the framework's own stated legal positioning for research purposes. It is not legal advice, and does not represent a settled reading of constitutional law — the underlying questions remain actively contested among legal scholars, courts, and policymakers.
